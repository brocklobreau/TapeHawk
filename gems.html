How a stock gets on this page

1. A story. A headline the classifier scored + (the Big News threshold) and read as positive, on a real listed equity, inside the last hours.

2. No move yet. The price at the headline is fixed — the grader's entry bar, or the first bar after the story, or the prior close for overnight news — and the move since is the test. Under ±% is quiet. Past % at any horizon and it's moved to the "already ran" list below: that one is a halt-chase, not a gem.

3. The checklist. Float tier from the same cache the Halts tab uses; the SEC offering check (shelves, PIPEs, priced deals); and the research panel's valuation read with the specific facts that fed it (P/E, book, sales, analyst target, distance from the 52-week low).

4. Spike odds. The site's own archive: every Big News headline it has graded in the last days in the same category and tone, and how many moved %+ within the hour. %+ reads "usually spikes", under % "usually nothing". Fewer than 8 graded stories in the category and it falls back to all big positive stories and says so. It is a base rate, not a prediction.

5. Safeguard. The reasons not to take it, each a measured fact: an offering or shelf on file, a large float, a value-trap read, a category that usually does nothing, a quiet story older than hours, or a headline that only just clears the Big News bar. A card that passes every check is a clean setup — not a buy signal.

6. Rank. Importance, plus points for quiet, low float, value and spike odds, minus points for anything on file at EDGAR or a category that usually does nothing. The reasons are printed on the card. Refreshes every seconds in the background — the page never waits on a data feed.

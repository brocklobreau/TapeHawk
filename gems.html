Live Halts SEC Filings Hidden Gems Scoreboard

The 20 best-scoring businesses the market isn't paying up for — and hasn't noticed yet. Every US-listed company over $300M, run through the same rules Bellwether's undervalued screen used: it has to be cheap on its own numbers, really profitable with a clean balance sheet, growing, and it must not have moved yet — anything in the top third of its 52-week range or up 35% in ten sessions is thrown out, not scored down. Its own arithmetic has to say there's a discount to collect, and quality throttles cheapness, because a stock is usually that cheap for a reason. Then the part Bellwether never had: a catalyst score — earnings beats, analyst upgrades, a trend that has started to turn, insiders buying, an activist 13D, buybacks — because cheap is the setup and a catalyst is what makes the market look. A cheap stock with nothing happening can sit cheap for years.

This is arithmetic on reported numbers, refreshed daily after the close. It narrows thousands of stocks to a handful that are cheap, sound and still early; some will re-rate, some will stay cheap for years. It can't see a lawsuit, a customer leaving, or a CFO resigning next week. Click a row for the reasoning, the fair-value math and the exact figures behind it.

How the score works

Vetoes first, not penalties. A name is out — at any score — if it has no usable P/E or is unprofitable; P/E is over 45; net margin is under 2%; free cash flow is negative; revenue is shrinking; debt is over twice equity; it sits above 65% of its 52-week range (the re-rating already happened); it is up more than 35% in the last ten sessions (the catalyst already fired); or its own fair-value math implies less than 15% upside (priced about right — a fine company, not an undervalued one). The reason is kept, not swallowed.

Cheap, 30%. P/E, EV/EBITDA, free-cash-flow yield and price-to-book, each banded 0–100 (P/E under 8 is 100, under 12 is 92, under 16 is 80, under 20 is 66, under 25 is 50, under 30 is 36). Then quality throttles it: the cheap score is multiplied by 0.35 + 0.65 × quality, so a P/E-of-6 name with poor quality keeps only about a third of its discount. A weighted sum would let an extreme discount drag a bad business up the list, which is precisely backwards.

Quality, 20%. Net margin (20%+ is 100), return on equity (20%+ is 100), free-cash-flow margin (15%+ is 100) and debt to equity (under 0.3 is 100).

Growth, 10%. Revenue growth (25%+ is 100, 12%+ is 86, 5%+ is 70, flat is 52) and earnings-per-share growth on the same shape.

Not moved yet, 15%. Lower in the 52-week range scores higher — the opposite of a momentum screen, on purpose — and a quiet last ten sessions (under 5% either way is 100) scores higher than a name that is starting to run.

Catalyst, 25%. Cheap is the setup; this asks whether anything is happening to make the market look. Six signals, each with evidence behind it, weighted 25 / 20 / 20 / 15 / 10 / 10: earnings beats in the last four quarters (4 of 4 is 95, 3 is 80, 2 is 55; a miss last quarter costs 15 — stocks drift for weeks after a beat, the most robust finding in the literature); net analyst upgrades in 90 days (+2 is 95, +1 is 78, 0 is 50, −2 is 12); the six-month trend (up 5–40% and above the 50-day average is best — value that has started to work beats value still falling, which scores 15–35); insider open-market buying in 180 days (two or more buyers is 95, one is 78, only sales is 40); an activist Schedule 13D on file from this site's own SEC watcher (100, else 40); and the share count year over year (down 3%+ is 95, any buyback 75, dilution over 2% is 20). Headline word-counting is not used.

Fair value. A fair P/E starts at 12× (a no-growth, average-margin business), adds 0.35× per point of revenue growth and 0.2× per point of margin over 8%, and is capped between 9× and 26×. What that multiple implies is blended 60/40 with the analyst consensus target and shown as a dollar figure. It is what the numbers imply today, not a forecast of when the market will agree.

Track record. Every symbol that enters the list is written down with that day's price and the S&P (SPY), then graded at one, three and six months against the S&P over the same window. A symbol is not re-recorded until 30 days after its last pick, so one stock can't fill the record with copies of itself. The split by catalyst score is the question the record exists to answer: does cheap-plus-catalyst beat cheap-alone? Until there are dozens of graded picks, treat the numbers as anecdotes.

Reading it. 75+ is deep value with quality behind it — rare. 62+ is undervalued and worth the work. 58+ is mildly cheap. Under 58 is not shown at all: a weak gem is not a gem. Figures come from the latest reported annual numbers and the current price, so a company that just reported will move the next day.

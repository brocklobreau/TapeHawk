wireprices—

Before the halt. A stock halts to the upside because it ran past its band — about 10% over its five-minute reference for most stocks above $3, 20% under $3 — and sat there for fifteen seconds. The headline saying so comes after. This page starts the clock at the story: every FDA approval, buyout, contract win, trial result, partnership or other positive Big News headline the wire delivers becomes a setup, its stock is watched tick by tick, and you see the move, the speed, and the distance to the band while the door is still open.

Prices are IEX, one exchange's slice of the tape, polled every two seconds; the band is an estimate. Nothing here places an order — you do, through your broker, or you don't. Most setups never move. The ones that do move fast.

The stories here come off the same stream the Live tab shows: the press-release wires only (GlobeNewswire, PR Newswire, and all four wires through rtpr). The Benzinga socket is off since October 2026, so nothing here is a rewrite of a release that came minutes earlier, and no "shares are trading higher" piece opens a setup.

How this works, and what it cannot do

What becomes a setup. A headline on one to three tickers that reads as FDA approval or clearance, a buyout or merger agreement, a contract or order win, positive trial data, a strategic partnership — or anything the Live tab already scores as Big News with a positive tone. Analyst notes, earnings, offerings, movers columns and "why is X up" pieces are left out. Halt headlines are not setups; they are the thing this page is trying to beat.

The numbers. since story is the latest trade against the price at the moment the story landed. last 60s is the speed. The band is the LULD limit: the five-minute average price seen so far, plus the band percentage for the stock's price tier (doubled in the first fifteen and last twenty-five minutes of the day). to band is how much further it has to go before a halt. A stock that sits at the band for fifteen seconds is paused for five minutes.

States. fresh: story landed, nothing yet. moving: up the alert percentage since the story. near: moving and within the alert distance of the band — this is the one. halted: the exchange said so, or a running stock went silent for a minute (the Benzinga "halted on circuit breaker" headline used to be the fastest sign; with that socket off, the exchange's table and silence are what is left). reopened: printed again after the halt, with the reopen price against the story. faded: a mover that gave it back. Setups are watched for thirty minutes, then filed below with how they ended.

The wires. Press releases are read straight from the wire services' own feeds (GlobeNewswire and PR Newswire by default, more with WIRE_FEEDS) every few seconds, and a release with a US ticker becomes a headline here — source named. They are the whole stream now: the Benzinga socket is off. When the same story arrives from two places the pair is matched (same ticker, within fifteen minutes) and the lag is scored against the one that came second; the same release delivered twice by one source (rtpr sometimes sends two article ids for one release) is marked a copy and counted once. Business Wire and Accesswire do not publish an open all-releases feed; that is what rtpr adds — all four wires over one socket, with RTPR_KEY set on the server and the "All articles" rule turned on in the rtpr dashboard. Each rtpr alert carries only a link, so the article is fetched before it is judged; the Live tab shows which pipe it came off ("rtpr · businesswire").

What it cannot do. It cannot see the whole tape: IEX is a slice, so a thin stock can look quiet here while it trades elsewhere, and the first prints of a spike can show up a tick late. It cannot know the exchange's exact reference price. It cannot tell you the reopen will be higher — Halthawk's backtest tab measures how often it is. And it cannot buy. The seconds between "moving" and "near" are yours.

Every trading halt on US exchanges, straight from Nasdaq's own halt feed — the authoritative record, not a headline about it. The reason code is the part that matters: LUDP is a five-minute volatility pause, T12 is an exchange asking the company questions and can run for days.

Once a halt has reopened and an hour has passed, it gets measured: the gap from the last price before the halt to the reopening print, then what the next 15 and 60 minutes did. Every halt is measured, not a selected few — that is the point. These are market-wide base rates to compare your own trades against, and they are descriptions of what happened, not predictions of what happens next.

News halts T1 → T2 → T3 is one halt, not three

T1News pending

Trading is halted pending the release of material news. The exchange knows something is coming; you do not. Nothing to read, no resumption time.

T2News released

The news has begun the dissemination process — that is Nasdaq's own wording. T2 means the wire has the story, not that it has reached your screen. There is a real gap, usually seconds, between the code flipping and the headline landing. Acting on the code alone is trading the fact that something was announced, which says nothing about direction.

T3News fully disseminated

Two clock times are now posted: when quotes may be entered and when trading resumes. This is the only state where you know both the news and the schedule.

A news halt reopens through the same auction as a volatility pause, but its auction collar is set off the last sale at the greater of $1.00 or 10% — not the ±5% of an LULD pause — so the reopening print can land much further from the halt price. On a $4 stock, $1 is 25%. Read what the news is before reading how it feels: on a small cap that has been running, it is often an offering.

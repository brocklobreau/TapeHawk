A halt makes this list when a Big News headline on the same ticker landed in the four hours before it or the half hour after — the wire and the exchange agreeing something happened. Most halts have no headline behind them; those are price moving on air, and they are hidden by default.

Every halt is still recorded and measured, story or not — the silent ones are the control. The comparison lives lower on the page, and it is what decides whether hiding them is right. Numbers are descriptions of what happened, not predictions of what happens next.

Halt codes T1 → T2 → T3, and what each code implies
T1News pending

Trading is halted pending the release of material news. The exchange knows something is coming; you do not. Nothing to read, no resumption time.

T2News released

The news has begun the dissemination process — Nasdaq's own wording. T2 means the wire has the story, not that it has reached your screen. Acting on the code alone is trading the fact that something was announced, which says nothing about direction.

T3News fully disseminated

Two clock times are now posted: when quotes may be entered and when trading resumes. The only state where you know both the news and the schedule.

A news halt reopens through the same auction as a volatility pause, but its collar is set off the last sale at the greater of $1.00 or 10% — not the ±5% of an LULD pause — so the reopening print can land much further from the halt price. On a $4 stock, $1 is 25%.
